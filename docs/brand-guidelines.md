# Brand Guidelines

This file is the practical brand guide for future design and content work on the new `Si Asha Foundation` website.

It is based on:

- the internal documents in `SI ASHA NEPAL (NP-EN)/`
- the public interim Nepal site at `https://siashafoundation.org/`
- the newer Spain-led site at `https://siasha.org/`
- the official brand manual at `/Users/alexf/Documents/fundaciones-ong/manual de marca FDH y Si Asha.pdf`

## Brand essence

### Core idea

`Asha` means `hope`.

The brand does not present hope as passive optimism. It presents hope as something built through care, structure, commitment, and action.

### Mission frame

Si Asha exists to make education, therapy, care, protection, and inclusion real for children and young people with intellectual and developmental disabilities in Nepal.

### Emotional promise

- dignity instead of invisibility
- care instead of abandonment
- structure instead of improvisation
- long-term accompaniment instead of one-off charity

### Brand character

- hopeful
- serious
- warm
- credible
- human
- committed
- non-paternalistic

## Positioning

The strongest current positioning across the source materials is:

- institutional enough to earn trust
- emotional enough to move people to act
- specific enough to avoid generic NGO messaging

The new site should feel like:

- a real organisation with governance, systems, and continuity
- a mission-led charity with emotional clarity
- a credible bridge between local work in Nepal and supporters abroad

The new site should not feel like:

- a startup
- a generic donation landing page
- a volunteer travel project
- an overly corporate foundation detached from the children and families it serves

## Audience groups

The website has to speak clearly to several audiences without losing coherence:

- donors making one-time contributions
- members making recurring contributions
- potential exclusive members / high-commitment supporters
- volunteers and collaborators
- institutions and companies
- families, partners, and local stakeholders
- trust-seeking visitors who need proof the organisation is real and well-run

## Messaging principles

### What to emphasize

- children and young people with intellectual and developmental disabilities
- education, therapy, and care as concrete rights and services
- long-term commitment
- local teams and women trained locally
- province-by-province expansion
- dignity, inclusion, and practical support
- trust and transparency

### What to avoid

- pity-first storytelling
- saviour language
- vague claims about changing the world
- startup or marketing jargon
- empty inspiration without operational substance
- excessive visual sweetness that weakens seriousness

### Preferred message pattern

The strongest pattern in the better materials is:

1. name a real problem clearly
2. show the concrete response
3. connect that response to dignity and future
4. ask the visitor to take a specific action

## Voice and tone

### Voice attributes

- compassionate but not sentimentalized
- direct but not aggressive
- elevated but still plain-language
- inspiring but grounded in reality

### Tone by context

Homepage hero:

- concise
- emotionally resonant
- action-oriented

Programme pages:

- concrete
- explanatory
- factual with human warmth

Transparency pages:

- plain
- rigorous
- institutional

Donation and membership flows:

- reassuring
- simple
- motivating
- low-friction

Team and story sections:

- human
- respectful
- specific

## Signature phrasing patterns already present

These are recurring brand lines and themes that can guide future copy:

- `Create hope. Take action.`
- `Hope is not something we wait for. It is built every day.`
- `When you say yes, the story changes.`
- `School by school, province by province.`
- `Every gesture matters.`
- `Because every child deserves dignity.`

Use these patterns as direction, not as text to repeat mechanically everywhere.

## Visual identity direction

### Overall look

The newer `siasha.org` site shows the strongest current visual direction:

- editorial charity look
- large emotional photography
- generous spacing
- strong red primary brand color
- warm orange accent for interaction and emphasis
- white backgrounds with clean sections
- mix of trust and emotion rather than dense institutional layout

For the new site, keep that emotional-direct style, but tighten the information architecture and trust signals.

### Design mood

- warm, not cute
- elegant, not luxurious
- human, not handmade-rustic
- modern, not trendy
- bright, not loud

## Corporate colors

The official brand manual is the primary authority for palette decisions. Live-site CSS values are useful implementation references but not the main source of truth.

### Official brand palette from the manual

- `#BD0833`
- `#EB6B2F`
- `#FFA952`
- `#9C5E03`
- `#E8CEB0`

### Verified live-site colors

- Primary foundation red: `#A80000`
  - from theme inline styles: `hsl(0, 100%, 33%)`
- Darker red hover/state: `#750000`
  - from theme inline styles: `hsl(0, 100%, 23%)`
- Elementor primary red: `#C10932`
- Warm accent / hover accent: `#FEA850`
- Near-black text: `#111111`
- Dark gray: `#333333`
- White: `#FFFFFF`

### Recommended usage model

Because the live site mixes close but inconsistent reds, future agents should normalize the web palette around the manual.

Recommended canonical palette for the new site:

- Brand primary: `#BD0833`
- Warm action accent: `#EB6B2F`
- Warm highlight accent: `#FFA952`
- Support earth tone: `#9C5E03`
- Warm neutral surface: `#E8CEB0`
- Text primary: `#111111`
- Text secondary: `#333333`
- Surface base: `#FFFFFF`
- Interactive darker red state: derive an accessible darker state from `#BD0833` if needed in the future codebase

### Color meaning

- Red carries commitment, action, seriousness, and brand recognition
- Orange carries warmth, invitation, and human energy
- White keeps the site breathable and credible
- Dark neutrals preserve readability and institutional tone

### Color usage rules

- Use red for the primary brand signal, main CTAs, links, and headings where appropriate
- Use orange sparingly for hover states, highlights, and selected emphasis moments
- Do not overuse orange as a second primary brand color
- Keep backgrounds mostly clean and light
- Avoid rainbow NGO palettes or excessive gradients

## Typography direction

The official brand manual gives explicit desktop-brand typography references, while the live sites show more mixed implementation details.

### Manual typography references

- body / principal: `Calibri`
- headings / secondary: `Century Gothic`

### Typography goals

- headlines should feel clear, strong, and emotionally charged
- body text should be highly readable and calm
- hierarchy should be obvious quickly

### Practical direction for the new site

- preserve the manual’s intent: highly readable body text plus clean geometric headings
- use a strong sans-serif or contemporary editorial pairing if web implementation needs better technical substitutes
- avoid playful or highly decorative fonts
- use large, confident headings
- keep body copy comfortable and open
- support bilingual content without typography breaking in Spanish or English

### Content rhythm

- short hero lines
- medium-length explanatory paragraphs
- frequent section headers
- strong CTA blocks
- evidence blocks with numbers and outcomes

## Photography and imagery

### Verified visual pattern

The stronger current brand presentation relies on:

- real documentary photography
- children, classrooms, therapies, families, and staff in context
- natural light and real environments
- portraiture with dignity

### Photography rules

- prefer real photos over illustrations
- show action, care, education, therapy, and presence
- avoid stock-photo NGO aesthetics
- avoid images that reduce children to symbols of suffering
- favor eye-level, respectful framing
- include women staff, teachers, and local leadership visibly
- preserve the warmth of the manual’s rounded, friendly visual language without making the site childish

### Image selection priorities

1. real programme activity
2. relationships and care
3. evidence of schools and therapeutic work
4. team and leadership portraits
5. geographic expansion context

## UI style principles

### Layout

- clean sections
- large imagery
- clear cards for programmes and actions
- repeated CTA rails are acceptable if disciplined
- numbers and outcomes should be visually prominent

### Components that fit the brand

- strong hero with one main message and two or three action paths
- impact stats band
- programme cards with real imagery
- trust/document cards for transparency
- testimonial or story cards grounded in real people
- location cards or map sections
- membership and donation modules with very clear pricing/amounts

### Components to avoid

- overly playful icon systems
- glassmorphism, neon, or trendy visual effects
- overly dense dashboards
- cluttered carousels as the primary way to communicate important content

## Conversion style

The brand asks for action often, but should do so with dignity.

### Primary conversion actions

- become a member
- become an exclusive member
- donate
- sponsor / apadrina
- volunteer
- contact

### CTA writing style

- direct
- positive
- specific

Prefer:

- `Become an Exclusive Member`
- `Donate Now`
- `Create hope. Take action.`

Avoid weak CTA language like:

- `Learn More` as the only action everywhere
- generic `Submit`
- abstract copy with no action cue

## Trust signals

Trust is part of the brand, not an afterthought.

The site should visibly support trust through:

- team and board presence
- transparency documents
- clear contact details
- programme specifics
- geographic reality
- membership and donation clarity
- legal/privacy clarity

## Bilingual and cross-market notes

The source materials span Nepal, Spain, and international audiences.

Design and content systems should therefore support:

- Spanish and English cleanly
- different donation/legal expectations by market if needed later
- a consistent brand voice across languages

Do not design a system that only works elegantly in one language.

## Brand architecture note

The brand manual makes the relationship explicit:

- `Fundación Si Asha` and `Familia de Hetauda` share the same visual identity system
- the difference is verbal positioning and audience focus

For the future official website, default to the `Fundación Si Asha` communication model:

- more institutional
- more rigorous
- more alliance-ready
- still human and hopeful

## Known inconsistencies to handle carefully

- The current live properties use slightly different institutional scopes
- The current live properties use mixed red values
- Some public pages still contain template leftovers and mixed-quality English labels

For the new site, preserve the strong emotional and visual direction, but remove those inconsistencies.

## Summary for design agents

If you are designing for this brand, aim for:

- hope with weight
- warmth with credibility
- emotion with structure
- conversion with dignity
- editorial charity design with strong real photography
- red-led identity with restrained orange warmth
